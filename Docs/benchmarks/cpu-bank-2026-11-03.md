# CPU round: the body's mode bank, 2026-11-03

0.16.0 against 0.15.2 (`eb15eb9`), one round. The numbers are development-
machine figures (an Intel Xeon vCPU at 2.8 GHz, 4 threads, GCC 13.3.0,
Release `-O3`), not Reason's or a DAW's CPU, and the machine is a shared VM:
wall-clock figures move by 10% between identical runs, which is why the
instruction counts below come first.

## What was slow

Instructions per sample in `AcukotoEngine::process()` (callgrind, the same
off-tree workload before and after: a strum over every string, then a pressed
melody, Performer at 48 kHz, 256-sample blocks; 23,808 samples):

| build | `process()` Ir | per sample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0.15.2 | 1.612 G | 67.7 k |
| single-precision bank | 1.250 G | 52.5 k |
| + diagonal string load | 1.197 G | 50.3 k |

In 0.15.2 the body's 356 modes took two sums per sample over the 26 bar
coordinates (the force on each mode, and the modes' pull on the bar), about
38 k instructions. The pull vectorized and the force did not (GCC 13 reported
"complicated access pattern" on the nested `std::array` indexing), and both
read their own double-precision table, 148 KB between them, from L2.

## What changed

- One shared single-precision table of participations serves both sums
  (37 KB), and each runs as a flat loop over every mode, which GCC 13 and
  Clang 18 both vectorize (four to a vector). The mode filters, which stay
  double, run two to a vector, and the four radiation sums are four
  vectorized dot products. States, filter coefficients and radiation weights
  stay double. The participations are rounded to single precision where the
  modes are built, so the junction mobility, the tuning and the bank use the
  same numbers.
- The strings' load, a diagonal, is applied as a diagonal instead of a dense
  26 x 26 product.

Against the build before the bank change, the two demos differ by at most
one 24-bit step (-124 and -126 dB re signal). The diagonal change is
byte-identical. `Acukoto.Tuning`, `Engine`, `DspFuzzReplay`, `DemoMidiSources`
and `AudioDemos` pass, and `AcukotoDSPCxx17` builds with `-Werror`.

## What it came to

Wall clock, alternating the two builds on an otherwise idle machine:

| measurement | 0.15.2 | 0.16.0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `Acukoto.CpuBudget`, Koto Hirajoshi, median of 3, two runs | 0.510, 0.526 | 0.355, 0.354 |
| same, Koto Hankumoijoshi | 0.519, 0.521 | 0.376, 0.338 |
| off-tree workload above, best of 3, three runs | 0.456, 0.502, 0.470 | 0.366, 0.326, 0.349 |

(Realtime factors: 0.35 is 35% of one core.) The worst 256-sample block stayed
between 1.0 and 1.9 times its 5.33 ms deadline in both builds, 1.14-1.48 before
and 1.02-1.86 after: it is dominated by something other than the bank (a VM
stall or a note-on block), and this round did not look into it.

## What still costs

Per sample of the 50.3 k, from a line-level callgrind profile taken between
the two changes: the two sums over the bank about 29 k, with their loop
control; the mode filters and radiation sums about 7 k; the dense `Y0` and
`(I + Y0 Zg)^-1` products about 4 k; the rest (strings, room, output stage)
about 10 k. The force sum loads and stores its accumulator for every
coordinate; taking the coordinates two at a time should save about 2 k
(estimated, not tried); neither is done in this round.

## Tried and dropped

Masking the room's delay lines instead of `%` is bit-exact, but the room is
0.00% of the profile (9 instructions per sample) and it was not committed.
