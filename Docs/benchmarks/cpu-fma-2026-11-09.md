# Acukoto CPU: fused multiply-adds — 2026-11-09

Acukoto 0.25.7 against 0.25.6 (`f8e66c4`). Two changes, both for CPUs with
AVX2 and FMA3 only:

1. the body's modal step and the junction's two matrix products use fused
   multiply-adds (`driveModesFma`, `pullOnBarFma`, `runModesFma`,
   `transposeApplyFma`);
2. the per-sample loop itself (the strings' filters, the junction's sums, the
   room) is compiled a second time for AVX2 and FMA3
   (`AcukotoEngine::processFused`), and `process()` takes it on such a CPU.

A CPU with AVX2 alone keeps 0.25.6's exact AVX2 kernels and its loop, and
every other CPU the portable code. No mode, coordinate, coefficient, control
period or level changed.

## Where the time goes

Callgrind on `AcukotoCpuProbe --preset 0 --oversampling 1 --scenario dense`
(0.3 s, only `AcukotoEngine::process` collected, `-O3 -g`): the modal step is
51% of the engine's instructions. Its drive from the 26 bar coordinates and
its pull back on them, 26 × 356 float products each per sample, are 24% on
their own lines and about 33% with their loop overhead and reductions; the
mode recursion is about 7% and the radiation sums 2%. The junction's two
26 × 26 products are 6%. The 52 string loops, the hand, the room and the
control-rate updates are the remaining 42%.

0.25.6's kernels are compiled `no-fma` so that they match the portable bank
to the bit, which costs each product a separate multiply and add. The fused
kernels keep every sum's order and lanes and round once per term instead of
twice. The strings' filters (Thiran, dispersion, loss sections) are short
chains of multiply-adds on paired polarisations; compiled for AVX2 and FMA3
they run as fused three-operand instructions (the compiler fuses where it
may, 367 fused operations in GCC 13's `processFused`).

## Measured CPU

GCC 13.3, Release (`-O3`), on a shared 4-vCPU Intel Xeon @ 2.10 GHz
container. `AcukotoCpuProbe --oversampling 1 --seconds 2 --scenario all`,
48 kHz, 64-frame callbacks, thirteen strings: five rounds, each running
0.25.6, change 1 alone and 0.25.7 in turn. Calling-thread CPU per callback;
the table gives the median over the five rounds of each round's median and
99th percentile (1500 blocks a case and round). A 64-frame block is
1333 µs. Development-machine figures only, never Reason or DAW CPU.

| Case | 0.25.6 median, µs (% of a core) | Change 1 alone | 0.25.7 | Reduction | 0.25.6 p99, µs | 0.25.7 p99 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hirajoshi, dense | 188 (14.1%) | 166 | 166 (12.5%) | 12% | 280 | 222 |
| Hirajoshi, gestures | 176 (13.2%) | 157 | 154 (11.6%) | 13% | 334 | 232 |
| Hankumoijoshi, dense | 192 (14.4%) | 170 | 145 (10.9%) | 24% | 282 | 227 |
| Hankumoijoshi, gestures | 207 (15.5%) | 179 | 157 (11.8%) | 24% | 347 | 277 |

The fused modal step takes 11-14% off every case. The fused loop takes a
further 12-15% off Hankumoijoshi and 0-2% off Hirajoshi. Single rounds
stray: one in five, in some case, ran 18-43% slow, for every version alike,
the shared machine's other tenants; the median of rounds sets them aside.
The 99th percentiles fell 20-31% but follow those tenants too, so worst-case
blocks still need a quiet machine to judge.

Clang 18 builds of 0.25.6 and change 1, two more pairs run while the unit
tests used another core: the first pair was mixed (−12% to +21% by case),
the second 10-16% lower in every case. Change 2 was not timed with Clang.

The project's goal of under 10% of a core on worst-case blocks at 1× is not
met: the medians are 10.9-12.5% and the 99th percentiles 17-21% here.

## The sound

A null test: the probe's raw float output (`--samples`), all four cases,
2 s each, 0.25.7 minus 0.25.6.

| Case | Signal RMS | Residual RMS | Residual peak | Residual under the signal |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hirajoshi, dense | −22.5 dBFS | −158.0 dBFS | −141.0 dBFS | 135.5 dB |
| Hirajoshi, gestures | −17.1 dBFS | −155.2 dBFS | −138.5 dBFS | 138.1 dB |
| Hankumoijoshi, dense | −22.4 dBFS | −158.3 dBFS | −141.0 dBFS | 135.9 dB |
| Hankumoijoshi, gestures | −16.9 dBFS | −155.6 dBFS | −138.5 dBFS | 138.7 dB |

Change 1 alone gives the same residuals to 0.1 dB; change 2 moves the output
from change 1's by at most −144.5 dBFS, under one 24-bit step, because the
strings' filters run in double precision.
Clang's null test of change 1 matches GCC's: 135-139 dB under the signal,
its peak −135 dBFS at most.

The largest difference is one 24-bit step (−138.5 dBFS), the rounding of the
float sums and nothing more: the change is inaudible by construction and by
measurement, so no listening test was run. The output is no longer the same
to the bit on every x86 CPU: an AVX2 CPU without FMA3 renders 0.25.6's bits,
one with FMA3 these. The unit, audio and perf tiers pass on this FMA3 CPU,
so through the fused path.

`Acukoto.ModalRadiation` keeps its bit-exact checks of the AVX2 kernels and
adds the fused ones: over every bank size, with and without a strike, each
output meets the portable bank within 1e-4 of its vector's largest
magnitude (1e-12 for the double-precision junction). A fused drive with one
coordinate left out fails it.
