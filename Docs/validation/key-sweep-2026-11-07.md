# Every key as even as the strings, 2026-11-07

This change starts from 0.22.0, commit `c50c7ad`. The owner asked for every
key to sound as even as the strings. The strings' own notes and the body hits
were already levelled (`StringSpec::touch`, `StrikeKey::touch`); a pressed
key and a moved-ji key took their string's touch, so they were not
(`scale-evenness-2026-11-03.md`, Findings not acted on). Two changes:

- **The hand follows the ji.** A moved ji's key was plucked 4.5 cm from the
  ryukaku whatever its speaking length. From about C6 up that length is under
  9 cm, so the pluck's share was held at its limit, 0.5: the string was
  plucked at its middle and lost its even partials. The pluck on a moved ji's
  key now lands at the share of the string's own open length that the place
  gives (`AcukotoEngine::pluck`), for the ordinary, near and away places
  alike. Pressed keys and the strings' own notes are plucked as before.
- **A touch per key.** `InstrumentSpec::keyTouch` scales the pluck's force on
  each key that no string's own note plays, on top of its string's touch
  (`Source/DSP/AcukotoKeyTouches.h`, written by `AcukotoLevels --write-keys`).
  Each takes its key 80% of the way, in dB, from its level without the touch
  to the strings' mean without theirs, the owner's rule for the strings and
  the hits (`Docs/decisions.md`, 2026-11-03). Touches run from 0.598 to
  2.484 (Koto Hirajoshi) and 0.618 to 2.260 (Koto Hankumoijoshi).

No loss, body, tuning, technique, hit or strike changed, and the strings' and
the hits' touches are as in 0.22.0: their levels did not move.

## Protocol

`AcukotoLevels` (written at the head of `Tools/MeasureLevels.cpp`): each key
alone at one velocity, from a reset engine at 48 kHz, 10 ms into the render,
with the preset's default controls (oshide on) and the room off; ITU-R
BS.1770-4 K-weighted mean square of both microphones over the 0.6 s after the
pluck, engine alone, before the Performer's output stage. Every key from the
lowest string, D3, up to C8 that is not a string's own note: 46 keys on each
preset. `Acukoto.Levels` (audio tier) fails when a string, a hit or a key
stands more than 0.5 dB from its intended level at velocity 0.7.

## Sample rate

0.25.0, the same day. The sweep was rendered again at 44.1 and 96 kHz
(`KeySweep --rate`, every key 50-108 at velocity 0.7, resampled to 48 kHz
before the same analysis) and set beside the 48 kHz sweep. The strings' own
notes agree across the rates within 1 cent in every window from 0.3 s on,
and within 0.5 dB in level up to C7. Above G7 they did not: at 44.1 and
48 kHz the keys from A7 (105) up sounded 4.5-5.5 dB quieter in the
fundamental at the onset than at 96 kHz and 12-17 dB quieter 0.3 s later,
and at 44.1 kHz G7 and A♭7 (103, 104) too. Rendered at 48, 50, 52, 54, 56,

## Not claimed

No listening verdict is claimed: nobody has heard the keys as a set. The
level target is the strings' mean at velocity 0.7, the owner's rule, not a
recorded koto's balance, and the keys stand further apart at other
velocities. A moved ji's key is still no koto's: a koto has thirteen strings,
and a ji moved past A5 makes strings shorter than a koto allows. Plucking them
at their string's share is a choice that keeps the tone continuous, not a
player's measured technique.
