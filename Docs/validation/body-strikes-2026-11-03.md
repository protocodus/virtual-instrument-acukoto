# Body strikes, 2026-11-03

0.17.0 against 0.16.0 (`86028af`). The keys from A0 to C♯3, which played a moved
ji, strike the body instead ([research](../research/body-strikes-2026-11-03.md)).
Nothing here is a listening verdict, and no recording of a strike was compared.

## Contact

One strike (C2, the near side) on Koto Hirajoshi, the hand's compression and
force traced each sample in a copy of the engine with a print added:

| velocity | arrival speed | contact | peak force |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0.3 | 0.053 m/s | 13.7 ms | 4.0 N |
| 0.7 | 0.19 m/s | 10.5 ms | 18.4 N |
| 1.0 | 0.50 m/s | 8.6 ms | 57.7 N |

The contact shortens as the speed rises, as a Hertzian contact does (time
goes as speed to the power -0.2: a 9.5 times faster hand gives 1.6 times
shorter contact, 13.7 to 8.6 ms). Traced at 1.07 m/s the same hand is in
contact for 7.4 ms with a peak of 142 N.

All 29 keys average -27.9 dB, against -28.1 for the four strings, and span
18.9 dB. The body answers best near the middle of its length: the stations
mirror about it (C♯1 and D1 give the same level within 0.1 dB and the same centre
of energy, 93 Hz), so about half the keys sound like another. The back stands 13.5 dB
over the near side and the top 9.1 dB over it at one hand speed. Nothing evens
them; a touch per surface or place, as for the strings, would, at the cost of
hand speeds that stray from one another by a factor of three.

A key's sound dies away: its level in 0.1-0.2 s against 1.5-1.6 s after the
strike falls 45.1, 51.5, 35.5, 46.3 and 44.9 dB on A0, C♯1, B1, A2 and C♯3.

## What the tests check

`Acukoto.Engine` (`checkStrikes`): every key below the lowest string is finite
and sounds (above -80 dB over the first 0.3 s), plucks no string, and renders
the same in blocks of 256 as in blocks of 1, 7, 64, 333, 2, 256 and 129; five
keys across the three surfaces die away by 30 dB or more in 3 s and stand at
least 10 dB higher at velocity 0.9 than at 0.3; two places on the body do not
sound alike. `Acukoto.Tuning` now reads every key from D3 up at its pitch and
requires the keys below to sound.

Two older tests changed with the behaviour. `Acukoto.Tuning` pitched the keys
below D3 (A0 and C2 among them); a hand has no pitch to read, so those keys are
now only required to sound. `Acukoto.Engine`'s 192 kHz case ran an extreme
lowered note on key 21 to guard the delay buffer; with A0's moved ji gone, the
lowest string (D3) takes its place, which needs about 2,800 cells against
14,800, and the buffer's size is unchanged.

## What stayed the same

The two demos render byte for byte as in 0.16.0 (`RenderAcukotoMidi` with
`--tail 1`, SHA-256 `b4c460c253b6a136` for Koto Hirajoshi and `4af93a8442a46a4d`
for Koto Hankumoijoshi, both builds): they use no key below D3. `Acukoto.Levels`
(strings only), `Acukoto.AudioDemos`, `Acukoto.Tuning`, `Acukoto.Engine`,
`Acukoto.DspFuzzReplay`, `Acukoto.DemoMidiSources` and `Acukoto.CpuBudget` pass,
and `AcukotoDSPCxx17` builds with `-Werror`; clang++ 18.1 compiles the DSP with
`-Wall -Wextra -Werror -fno-exceptions`.

## CPU

With no hand in contact a strike costs one branch per sample in `process()` and
two in `runModes`. Alternating the 0.16.0 and 0.17.0 builds on the workload of
the [0.16.0 benchmark](../benchmarks/cpu-bank-2026-11-03.md) (best of 3, three
runs each) gave realtime factors of 0.379, 0.352 and 0.309 for 0.16.0 and 0.362,
0.334 and 0.403 for 0.17.0: the machine moves by more than that between
identical runs, so no difference is claimed. `Acukoto.CpuBudget` read 0.38 and
0.41 on the two demos (0.16.0: 0.34-0.38). Instruction counts were not taken.
During a contact, which lasts 2 to 14 ms, a strike adds a 356-mode multiply-add
and a 356-mode dot product per sample; that was not timed separately.

## Not claimed

The sound is not compared with a recording of a hand on a koto: none is
available, and the source gives no force, contact time or place. The hand's
mass, stiffness and restitution, and its speeds, are estimates. A palm, a
knuckle and a hard rap were rendered as a lettered set for the owner to choose
by ear ([decisions](../decisions.md)).
