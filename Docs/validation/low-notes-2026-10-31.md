# The low notes against recordings (2026-10-31)

Acukoto 0.12.0 against 0.13.0, preset Koto Hirajoshi. Two changes are
compared: the radiation's quadrature, corrected, and the arched top coupled
to the box's bending. The owner reported that the lower tones did not sound
realistic.

## Protocol

Every string was plucked alone by `RenderAcukotoNotes 0 <dir> --seconds 2`
and measured by `Tools/measure_koto_notes.py --dir`. The result was set
beside the five professional recordings (Rokudan, Godan, Kudan, Kumoi and
Omoigawa; `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`) with `--compare`, by the protocol at the
head of that tool. The recordings are not committed.

A second, wider check took the 18 tracks of the CC BY-NC-SA archive.org item
*A Happy New Year from Japan, the Soul of Koto* and Etsuko Chida's *Koto
Kumiuta* (CC BY-NC): 1,388 isolated notes. Each string was rendered at five
seeds through a probe copy of the engine. The comparison was each partial's
early level (10-60 ms) against the power sum of partials 1-10, and each
partial's decay.

## The radiation's quadrature

Each mode radiates through a complex weight W, which the Kirchhoff grid finds
at the mode's own frequency. Until 0.12.0 the quadrature part was applied as
-w_m Im W times the mode's velocity. That equals j w W only at w = w_m: below
it, each mode's quadrature is w_m / w times too large.

The force at every ji was logged, sample by sample, and re-radiated offline.
Applying W at every frequency reproduces the engine's output to -38 dB. At
D3's second to fifth partials it matches within 2 dB. At D3's fundamental the
engine was 12 dB louder than its modes radiate. D3's force at the ji is flat
across its partials (heave 46.4, 45.2 and 43.2 dB at H1 to H3), so the
string is not the cause.

Against the full sum (W evaluated at each frequency on the same grid), in
third octaves over every ji and both microphones:

| | 50-63 Hz | 126-159 Hz at D3's ji | 200 Hz-5 kHz | above 5 kHz |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -w_m Im W (0.12.0) | +6 to +9 dB | +4 to +5 dB | within 1.7 dB | +2 to +3 dB |
| W kept at every frequency (0.13.0) | within 1 dB | -2.5 to -2.8 dB | within 1.6 dB | +3 to +4 dB |

0.13.0 keeps W through a 90-degree phase network on the output. It is two
chains of six first-order all-passes per channel, within 0.65 degrees from
15 Hz to 20 kHz at 48 kHz and above. It costs nothing per mode.

## The arched top

The top is arched along the body. A curved shell that moves out is stretched
along, so the box's bending strains the top and drives the walls. The
coupling comes from the top's longitudinal strain energy. It uses paulownia's
E_L, the top's thickness and the arch already in the model. It leaves out
the top's own give along the body, so it is the coupling's upper bound.

| Mode | 0.12.0 | 0.13.0 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| air, both holes in phase | 86.3 Hz, monopole 0.219 | 85.3 Hz, 0.199, now 13% bending |
| first bending | 94.5 Hz, monopole 0.005 | 94.4 Hz, 0.097 |
| shell's first breathing | 293.1 Hz | 299.2 Hz |

Coaldrake's acoustic camera found the koto's sound dominated by the 84 Hz air
mode and the 99 Hz first bending (ICA 2019). Before 0.13.0 the model's
bending was silent. Each low pluck now rings the body at 85-94 Hz. In the
low-notes phrase (`RenderAcukotoPhrase`, strings 1 to 5 and the keys below
D3), the 63 Hz octave sits 41 dB under the 500 Hz octave, where it sat 58.
In fifteen of the recordings it sits 21 to 55 dB under.

## What it does not close

D3's fundamental is still 22 dB weaker against its second partial than
recorded, and it now dies 5.4 times as fast. The recorded target is thin:
three D3 notes in the five recordings. Across the 18 tracks there are 37 D3
to E-flat 3 notes, with H1 against H2 ranging from -33 to +4 dB between
tracks, and 18 of them from one modern track. Nothing else in the body as
modelled radiates at 147 Hz from string 1's ji, which sits 1.6 cm inside the
side wall's mid-line, where the shell hardly moves. The ji moved to the
middle of the top radiates 150-200 Hz 10-23 dB more.

Twelve variants were tried on 0.11.2, before either change. None of them
lifted D3's fundamental more than 4 dB: radiation loss, box Q 30 and 140,
strings 10% closer together, walls at 0.5, 0.6 and 0.85 of full thickness,
potential-flow added mass, the microphones placed closer, over either end
and overhead, and the floor removed. What remains untried is the floor's own
motion: the legs and the tail driving the boards the koto lies on, which
would need estimates for the floor.

The listening set (A 0.12.0, B the radiation alone, C both) was sent to the
owner as `listening-tests/2026-10-31-low-notes/` in the project files. It is
not committed.

## Pressed keys between the modes

After 0.13.0 the Rack adapter's checks found two keys 12 dB weaker at their
fundamental than in 0.12.0: A-flat 4, G4's string pressed a semitone, and B5,
A5's string pressed a whole tone. The same keys on a moved ji, oshide off,
fall as far. Every key from C3 to E6 was rendered through `Performer` with
Koto Hirajoshi's defaults, velocity 90, and measured on the left channel: the
peak within 1.2% of the key's pitch, Hann over 50-350 ms.

| Key | 0.12.0 | radiation alone | 0.13.0 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A-flat 4, 415 Hz | 35.2 dB | 35.0 dB | 22.8 dB |
| A-flat 5, 831 Hz | 39.9 dB | 40.1 dB | 34.7 dB |
| B5, 988 Hz | 39.4 dB | 39.4 dB | 27.2 dB |

The radiation fix moves no key above 220 Hz by more than 1 dB; the arched
top moves these. At G4's ji the body's modes at 401.7, 407.3 and 416.7 Hz
moved to 393.1, 403.3 and 425.9 Hz, and the ji's mobility at 415 Hz fell from
1.30e-3 to 4.4e-4 m/s/N: A-flat 4 now falls in the dip between two modes. At
A5's ji the mobility at 988 Hz is unchanged (4.7e-3 and 5.1e-3 m/s/N), but the
wall modes beside it, at 978.5 and 986.9 Hz (now 974.3 and 986.0 Hz), lost their net volume (monopole
-0.015 and -0.024, now 0.005 and 0.003). B5's fundamental also beats, with a
null 0.2-0.35 s after the pluck.

Key to key, from B3 to E6, each fundamental less the median of the four keys
around it varies 4.9 dB RMS in 0.12.0 and 6.0 dB in 0.13.0. Three keys sit
more than 10 dB under: A-flat 4 (17), B-flat 5 (15) and B5 (11), where 0.12.0
had B-flat 5 alone (20). By the balance `measure_koto_notes.py` takes, each
pitch less the median of the pitches within two semitones, the model varies
8.3 dB RMS in 0.12.0 and 8.8 dB in 0.13.0. The eleven recordings with five or
more such pitches vary 3.6 to 11.9 dB, 8.4 dB pooled over 124 pitches, and 9% of their
pitches sit more than 10 dB under (the model's 10% and 13%).

So the dips are not a fault: a body puts some notes between its modes, and
these modes are the model's estimates. Which notes it hits depends on the
mode frequencies, and the arch's coupling is the upper bound, so no key is
moved off a dip by choosing where the modes fall.

## More recordings (2026-11-01)

The owner sent three producer loops and pointed to the Stanford gagaku page's
koto examples (<https://gagaku.stanford.edu/en/strings/koto/>, Reigakusha, ©
Rose/Kapuscinski). Both were measured by `measure_koto_notes.py` with
`--min-gap 0.25`, since none of them leaves 0.6 s between notes. Neither is
committed.

None of the loops can set the model. The melody in D at 114 bpm is
high-passed: it holds nothing below 100 Hz (-90 dB) and is 60 dB down at 125
Hz, so D3 and the fundamentals of G3 and below are filtered away. The guitar
and koto loop in F minor is one F3 eight times over a bass, rising in 4.5 ms
against the recordings' 40 ms. The loop in G at 140 bpm has five notes from
A-flat 3 to E-flat 4 with five times the classical recordings' inharmonicity
and fundamentals that die three times as fast.

The gagaku examples are a gakuso, unfiltered (their spectrum below 200 Hz sits
where Rokudan's does), and agree with the five classical recordings above the
bottom string. The balance of their isolated notes is -1 dB on A3 (one note),
+2 dB on B3 (two) and +1 dB on D4 (three), against the classical -2.8, -5.0
(B-flat 3) and +3.0 dB and the model's -15.8, -16.2 and +1.5 dB. So the thin
A3 and B-flat 3 are confirmed by a second koto.

Their tsumu example plucks strings 5 and 7, E3 and A3 at A = 430 Hz, together.
From 50 ms to 1 s after the pluck, E3's fundamental at 161 Hz sits 34-36 dB
under its second partial, while its third partial is there at -17 to -21 dB. A
real koto's lowest string can be thinner than the model's D3, so the three
classical D3 notes are not the only target for it.
